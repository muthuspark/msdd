#### Anti-Patterns Verdict

**LLM assessment:** This does not read as generic AI dashboard UI. The editorial serif/sans pairing, flat warm surfaces, single-purpose right rail, and quiet tab treatment form a distinct reading environment. The risk is not decoration. It is under-signalled state: the page has three navigation systems, but only two clearly communicate where the user is.

#### Overall Impression

The structure is substantially better than a dropdown outline. It now feels like a focused document workspace: feature context at the top, artifacts below it, prose left, guidance right. The highest-value next step is making the rail behave like navigation rather than a static list, so it continuously confirms the reader’s current location.

#### What's Working

- The full-width feature title gives the document a stable identity before the reader reaches content.
- Explore, Spec, and Tasks are correctly co-located. They are related artifacts, so presenting them as tabs removes unnecessary sidebar scanning.
- The desktop right rail earns its space. It supports scanning without competing with the body copy, and the restrained treatment fits the editorial visual system.

#### Priority Issues

**[P1] The outline does not visibly track the current section.**

- **Why it matters:** On a long specification, the reader loses orientation after scrolling. The rail looks available but does not answer the immediate question, “Where am I?”
- **Fix:** Use `IntersectionObserver` within the reader scrollport to mark the nearest visible heading with `aria-current="location"`, a dark text state, and one non-decorative 1px top/bottom or full-surface cue. Do not introduce a colored side stripe.
- **Suggested command:** `impeccable harden src/viewer`

**[P1] Switching artifacts discards reading context.**

- **Why it matters:** Explore, Spec, and Tasks are often consulted back and forth. Returning to the top after every tab change turns a quick comparison into repeated scrolling.
- **Fix:** Store the reader scroll offset per feature artifact for the browser session and restore it when returning to a tab. Reset to the top only for a first visit.
- **Suggested command:** `impeccable harden src/viewer`

**[P2] The feature title is friendly but lossy.**

- **Why it matters:** Title-casing a folder name can alter intentional casing in acronyms, version markers, or product names. The removed path improves calmness, but it removes disambiguation when feature names collide.
- **Fix:** Preserve display casing from source where available. Put the full relative feature path in a compact `title` attribute or a visually subtle, keyboard-accessible disclosure rather than restoring a persistent path row.
- **Suggested command:** `impeccable clarify src/viewer`

**[P2] The mobile navigation hierarchy will become crowded under a long title.**

- **Why it matters:** Title, tabs, and the “On this page” control all occupy the top region. At 200% zoom or with long feature names, the popover’s fixed offset can feel detached from its trigger.
- **Fix:** Anchor the mobile popover relative to the sticky header boundary, allow the title to wrap to two lines, and keep the tab row horizontally scrollable with a visible fade or scroll affordance.
- **Suggested command:** `impeccable adapt src/viewer`

**[P3] Error recovery is too generic for a file reader.**

- **Why it matters:** “Unable to read this Markdown file” tells the reader little about what failed or how to proceed.
- **Fix:** Name the artifact that failed, retain the feature tabs, and offer a retry control beside the error.
- **Suggested command:** `impeccable clarify src/viewer`

#### Persona Red Flags

**Alex (Power User):** The layout is efficient once loaded, but switching from Spec to Tasks and back loses the previous reading location. There are no shortcut keys for next artifact or outline navigation, so repeated cross-reference work remains mouse-heavy.

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent User):** The rail has a labelled nav and the mobile popover has Escape, which is a solid base. But the active outline location is not announced, focus does not have a persistent reading-location companion after scroll, and a long title must remain readable at high zoom without clipping.

**Jordan (First-Timer):** The feature-level sidebar and artifact tabs are much easier to understand than three separate file links. However, a first-time reader is not told what Explore, Spec, and Tasks mean, nor what the right rail does when the document has many headings.

#### Minor Observations

- The outline heading uses useful editorial spacing. Keep that treatment when adding active state.
- Tabs should keep a 44px effective hit area on touch, even if their visual padding remains compact.
- The right rail’s max-height should preserve its own scroll when a document has many sections; do not allow it to extend beyond the reader pane.

#### Questions to Consider

- Should the reader optimize primarily for deep sequential reading, or rapid cross-reference between Explore, Spec, and Tasks?
- Is the feature title intended as a humanized display name, or must it preserve folder casing exactly for technical accuracy?
- On mobile, should the outline be a simple list popover, or should it also show the currently active section before the reader opens it?
